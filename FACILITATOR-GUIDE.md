# Facilitator guide: Southbank Ready

## Purpose

Help newly arrived Southbank apartment residents turn basic flood awareness into a practical building plan and a few local connections. Learners see a short introduction, select **Get started**, then choose the resident/renter or supported path on the next page. Both paths include short scenarios, personal reflection and a building-walk step.

The opening flood photograph and first-chapter photo activities show past events in Werribee and Melbourne CBD, with dates and credits. They do not show current conditions or Southbank flooding. Use the revealable photo questions as optional discussion prompts; do not copy the behaviour of people or vehicles pictured near the water. The apartment and equipment illustrations are generic learning aids, so connect them to verified features during the real building walk.

This is an independent teaching resource. It does not establish a building's actual emergency procedure. Confirm that procedure with the people responsible for the building before use.

## Prepare the building details

Complete Facilitator setup with your building team:

- Building identity and address; emergency contact and after-hours arrangements.
- Orientation meeting location, clearly distinguished from an emergency assembly or refuge location.
- Flood-specific procedure, early-departure arrangements and who shares updates.
- Verified accessible exits and alternatives if lifts stop.
- Whether designated assistance/waiting points exist, which hazards they cover, and how people communicate their exact location to trained staff.
- Which doors, lifts, intercoms, alarms, pumps and power outlets have backup power, and its limitations.
- Whether evacuation equipment exists, who is trained, and its limits. Do not promise carrying or lift access that has not been assessed.
- Live booking URL, plus a separate supported-orientation URL if needed.
- Date checked. Recheck after changes to staffing, equipment or plans.

Confirm current VICSES and VicEmergency guidance. The included flood map is from the supplied July 2021 guide. Link to a newer official guide if one becomes available; update the map and description together.

## Resident / renter walk — proposed 30 minutes

| Time | Activity | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 min | Welcome and optional introductions | Say who you are, invite people to share a first name if comfortable, and explain the walk. |
| 5–12 min | Find information and exits | Show the building's plan, normal exits, assembly arrangements and the source of official updates. |
| 12–18 min | Basement and power discussion | Explain early storage preparation, restricted access during flooding, lifts, powered doors and service outages. Stay in safe, permitted areas. |
| 18–25 min | One shared scenario | “Heavy rain, a warning, and the car-park ramp is taking water.” Identify the safe next step and who to contact. |
| 25–30 min | Questions and neighbour connection | Invite voluntary check-in buddies. Agree unanswered follow-ups and show the after-hours contact. |

Friendly tone: casual conversation, no scores, optional introductions, no forced disclosure. If refreshments or social time are offered, confirm them with the organiser before advertising them. Do not demonstrate by entering wet or restricted spaces.

## Supported orientation — proposed 45 minutes

Offer a smaller group or individual appointment. Let the person choose whether to bring a carer, friend, interpreter or other support. Ask about access and communication preferences privately through an appropriate booking service; a diagnosis is not needed to request an adjustment.

| Time | Activity | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 min | Set the pace together | Confirm consent, preferred communication, breaks, privacy and the person's priorities. |
| 10–20 min | Check the actual route | Show entrances, accessible exits, door operation, and any designated assistance locations under the building's verified plan. |
| 20–30 min | Power interruption conversation | Explain what fails, what backup covers, who communicates changes and how early relocation can be arranged. Refer medical/equipment questions to the relevant provider. |
| 30–40 min | Personal assistance and contacts | Start or review an individual emergency evacuation plan with the building team and chosen supports. Agree backups when a carer cannot attend. |
| 40–45 min | Recap and follow-up | Provide the agreed information in the person's preferred format, confirm outstanding actions and invite optional introductions. |

Do not ask neighbours to improvise lifting or carrying. Evacuation chairs and other specialist equipment require appropriate training and a suitable plan. Do not assume a generic stairwell is a safe refuge. The individual leads decisions about their needs and information.

## Online booking setup

The default form demonstrates selecting a walk type, date, time and an optional access discussion preference. It explicitly saves only a local practice selection. Sample dates are generated relative to the current Melbourne date; they are not advertised real sessions. The calendar file also says PRACTICE ONLY and uses tentative status.

For real use, add an organiser booking link. The module sends the learner to that service after completing a path. Configure real capacity, locations, duration, reminders, cancellations, consent, accessibility requests and confirmation in the booking service. This module cannot confirm that a booking was completed externally.

## Learner outcomes to review

- Recognises that living high up does not eliminate service/access disruption.
- Finds official warnings and understands that a planning map is not a safe route.
- Chooses essential kit items and identifies why the distractors are unsafe or impractical.
- Knows to move basement valuables before flooding, and stay out once water enters.
- Knows 000 for immediate danger and 132 500 for flood and storm assistance.
- Has a personal plan or knows which unanswered questions to take to the building team.
- In the supported path: identifies relevant access, communication, power and support needs, with backup arrangements.

Use feedback from students, renters, people with disability and carers to improve wording and the actual session arrangements. Completion indicates that the learner interacted with the learning material; it is not proof of evacuation capability.

## Languages at the welcome and building walk

The module offers English, Bahasa Indonesia, Arabic, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese and Italian. Learners can switch using the Language menu without losing activity answers or notes. Arabic uses a right-to-left layout. Plan exports use the chosen language.

Before community rollout, have speakers of each language review the safety terminology, warning-level names and local expressions. These translations have not had an independent professional review. Keep official emergency numbers and the English warning-level names in brackets recognisable when refining wording.

The original VICSES map/PDF and external services may be in English. The module translates the map explanation; it does not relabel the official map. Organiser-supplied building information and personal notes remain in the language entered. Provide translated, verified building instructions or suitable language support for the walk where needed; the booking provider should state which language assistance is actually available.


## Quiz evaluation and printed take-home plan

Ask learners to complete the final check before choosing their building walk. It repeats all scenario questions and the emergency kit, then compares recorded first attempts with the latest final check. A perfect score is not required; use misunderstandings as conversation starters at the walk.

If you want shared evaluation results, run the Node/SQLite server and invite learners to opt into anonymous sharing before they begin. The organiser dashboard shows original choices, retries and final rounds, with CSV export. See RESULTS-GUIDE.md for setup, consent, incomplete baselines and backups. Do not interpret browser IDs as verified individuals or the learning results as evacuation certification.

Invite learners to print the A4 poster from My plan. They may print it blank or include notes. Long notes continue on extra pages. Remind people to choose which contact or support details they are comfortable displaying on a wall. Confirm building contacts, accessible exits and assistance arrangements during the session.
